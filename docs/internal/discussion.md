Limitations. Our study is conducted on a single dataset and domain (in-cabin driver monitoring); while this domain is representative of AIoT's negative-dominant deployment condition, findings may not transfer to domains with different background statistics or object density. We test three architectures at their smallest ("nano") variants; larger variants within the same families may exhibit different sensitivity given their greater capacity. Furthermore, each architecture and ratio configuration is evaluated via a single training run (one run per ratio per model, without repeated random seeds) to manage compute budgets; our results therefore reflect point estimates rather than empirical distributions, precluding formal statistical hypothesis testing (e.g., ANOVA or interaction tests) and leaving open the possibility that some observed variance stems from stochastic training dynamics rather than purely architectural differences. Our hard-negative mining protocol uses a single confidence threshold τ and a single baseline checkpoint per architecture; more sophisticated online or iterative mining strategies were outside this paper's scope but represent a natural extension. Finally, our deployment-cost estimates (Section IV-C) are derived analytically from FP-per-frame rates rather than measured on physical edge hardware, and should be read as directional estimates rather than validated power/bandwidth measurements.
